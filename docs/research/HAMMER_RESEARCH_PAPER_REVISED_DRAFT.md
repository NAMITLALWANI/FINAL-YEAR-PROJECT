## Abstract

Scholarly publication discovery and academic analytics face persistent data quality degradation caused by inconsistent bibliographic schemas across disparate metadata repositories, pervasive record duplication, and severe author name ambiguity. While standalone record linkage algorithms and offline machine learning disambiguation toolkits exist, their integration into operational scholarly infrastructure frequently suffers from error propagation, opaque machine-learned false merges, and hallucinated attributions from generative language models.

In this paper, we present **HAMMER** (*Hybrid Academic Metadata Aggregator and Evidence-Guided Author Entity Resolution System*), an auditable, human-in-the-loop software architecture that harmonizes multi-source bibliographic ingestion, deterministic publication deduplication, multi-signal author identity candidate retrieval, and provenance-grounded contextual synthesis. HAMMER enforces a strict architectural boundary between an authoritative relational system of record (SQLite) and rebuildable downstream dense vector indices (ChromaDB). 

We evaluate HAMMER across four independently audited empirical benchmarks:
1. **Publication Deduplication:** On an adjudicated 50-pair challenge benchmark exhibiting edition conflicts, subtitle truncations, and author-permutation variations, our proposed Deduplication Engine (V2)—featuring hard DOI conflict vetoes, edition guards, multi-year divergence vetoes, and prefix-aware matching—achieved **100.0% precision (33/33)** and **100.0% recall (33/33)** across 48 decided pairs with **zero false positive merges**, resolving the critical false merges generated by legacy weighted-similarity baselines.
2. **Author Candidate Generation:** On a 100-query sealed benchmark containing 56 gold-grounded researcher profiles, candidate harvesting achieved **100.0% harvesting coverage** (zero retrieval dropouts), surfacing the ground-truth OpenAlex identifier at Rank 1 in **100.0% of confirmed queries (50/50)** and **92.86% of all gold queries (52/56)** with a Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) of **0.9286**.
3. **Deterministic Entity Resolution:** Applying non-linear score, margin, and dominance gates over five extracted evidence signals, the resolver automatically resolved 13 identities with **100.0% precision (13/13)**, while safely referring 52 ambiguous cases to human adjudication and marking 35 as unresolved, completely eliminating catastrophic false merges.
4. **Semantic Retrieval and Synthesis Defense:** In a 20-query semantic retrieval benchmark, an empirical cosine similarity floor of $\tau = 0.35$ eliminated **100.0% of out-of-domain and gibberish false acceptances** while retaining complete in-corpus recall. A zero-trust citation containment validator enforces that all claims generated by an integrated Large Language Model strictly cite retrieved document identifiers, successfully intercepting fabricated citations.

HAMMER demonstrates that combining deterministic, precision-first decision gates with human-in-the-loop fallback mechanisms yields an operational academic intelligence platform that provides reliable data integrity without sacrificing discoverability.

**Keywords:** Scholarly Metadata Ingestion, Entity Resolution, Author Name Disambiguation, Publication Deduplication, Dense Semantic Retrieval, Citation Containment, Human-in-the-Loop Systems.

## 1. Introduction

Digital scholarship increasingly relies on automated aggregation systems to index the burgeoning scientific literature across distributed digital libraries, preprint repositories, and indexing services. Platforms such as Google Scholar, OpenAlex, Crossref, DBLP, and arXiv provide Application Programming Interfaces (APIs) and bulk data feeds that index millions of academic publications. However, constructing a unified, dependable knowledge graph from these heterogeneous data streams presents three formidable technical challenges:

1. **Schema Heterogeneity and Record Fragmentation:** Different metadata aggregators maintain divergent bibliographic representations. Title capitalization, subtitle formatting, punctuation, author name ordering, and persistent identifier availability (e.g., Digital Object Identifiers [DOIs], arXiv IDs) vary substantially across sources. Consequently, identical scholarly works are indexed as disparate records, fracturing citation metrics and confounding discovery queries.
2. **Author Name Ambiguity (Homonymy and Polysemy):** Author entity resolution represents an inherently ill-posed inverse problem. Common names (e.g., "Michael Jordan", "Wei Zhang") are shared by hundreds of distinct researchers worldwide (polysemy), while individual researchers frequently publish under varied name forms, typographical initials, married surname transitions, and transliterated variants (homonymy). Fully autonomous machine learning clustering systems often make catastrophic false merges—conflating distinct careers into a single profile—which severely compromises institutional attribution, tenure review, and bibliometric research.
3. **Hallucinatory Risks in Modern Generative AI:** Recent attempts to deploy Large Language Models (LLMs) and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) within academic search interfaces frequently introduce subtle factual inaccuracies, fabricating citations, misattributing findings, and generating plausible-sounding but ungrounded scholarly claims.

To address these challenges without sacrificing transparency or data safety, this paper introduces **HAMMER** (*Hybrid Academic Metadata Aggregator and Evidence-Guided Author Entity Resolution System*). HAMMER is designed around three fundamental design tenets:
- **Authoritative vs. Derived Boundary:** A transactional relational database (SQLite) serves as the immutable, authoritative system of record for publication identity, candidate author pools, and resolution provenance. Downstream dense vector indices (ChromaDB) and generative models are treated strictly as derived, rebuildable artifacts that never dictate primary entity identity.
- **Precision-First Safety Through Abstention:** In entity resolution, false positive merges (erroneously combining distinct entities) are substantially more damaging than false negative splits (treating identical entities as distinct). HAMMER incorporates strict non-linear margin and dominance gates that automatically resolve identities only when evidence is overwhelmingly definitive, routing ambiguous cases to an interactive human-in-the-loop review interface.
- **Zero-Trust Syntactic Attribution:** To prevent generative hallucinations in publication synthesis, HAMMER couples structured prompt constraints with a deterministic citation containment validator that filters any generated claim citing unverified or out-of-context document identifiers.

## 2. Related Work

### 2.1 Classical Record Linkage and Publication Deduplication
The mathematical foundations of record linkage were formalized by Fellegi and Sunter [1], who formulated optimal decision rules for classifying candidate record pairs into matches, non-matches, and uncertain pairs based on log-likelihood ratios of multi-attribute agreement. In scholarly digital libraries, publication deduplication has historically relied on exact identifier matching (DOIs, PubMed IDs) augmented by heuristic string similarity metrics (Jaro-Winkler, Levenshtein distance, n-gram token overlap). However, naive weighted string similarity frequently breaks down when confronting subtle boundary conditions, such as successive textbook editions (e.g., Sutton and Barto's *Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction*, 1st vs. 2nd edition) or companion papers sharing major title prefixes. HAMMER builds on Fellegi-Sunter principles by introducing deterministic veto guards—including explicit DOI conflict detection, edition keyword extraction, and multi-year divergence limits—that veto erroneous merges regardless of superficial string similarity.

### 2.2 Author Name Disambiguation (AND)
Author name disambiguation has evolved from rule-based heuristic clustering to sophisticated supervised and self-supervised machine learning architectures. The S2AND benchmark [2] unified heterogeneous author datasets, demonstrating that pairwise gradient-boosted trees trained on rich contextual features (coauthor graphs, affiliation similarity, venue overlap, and title embeddings) significantly outperform unconstrained clustering. More recently, the WhoIsWho benchmark and toolkit [3] highlighted the vulnerability of web-scale neural disambiguation models to sparse metadata and split institutional profiles. Unlike purely autonomous offline clustering frameworks that force global partitionings over frozen graphs, HAMMER operates as an online, human-in-the-loop system. It decouples candidate generation from identity resolution, scoring five explainable evidence dimensions while providing full provenance auditing and explicit abstention mechanisms.

### 2.3 Semantic Information Retrieval in Digital Libraries
Keyword-based lexical search (BM25, TF-IDF) frequently fails in academic literature discovery due to the vocabulary mismatch problem: specialized domains frequently describe identical phenomena using distinct technical nomenclatures. Dense bi-encoder architectures, such as Sentence-BERT [7], map publication titles and abstracts into low-dimensional semantic embedding spaces where semantic similarity corresponds to cosine proximity. Dense vector databases, such as ChromaDB [8], enable low-latency approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) retrieval over large document corpora. HAMMER integrates bi-encoder dense embeddings (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`) within a rebuildable ChromaDB index, using an empirical similarity threshold floor to reject out-of-domain and malformed queries.

### 2.4 Retrieval-Augmented Generation and Citation Faithfulness
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) [4] couples neural passage retrieval with pre-trained generative language models to produce grounded synthesis summaries. Benchmark frameworks such as KILT [5] established rigorous standards for evaluating knowledge grounding through passage provenance and citation attribution. However, recent studies demonstrate that generative models often exhibit "citation hallucination"—generating factual-sounding claims that reference irrelevant or non-existent document IDs. HAMMER addresses this vulnerability through a deterministic citation containment validator that enforces syntactic membership verification over model-generated claims prior to UI presentation.

### 3.1 Authoritative Relational Persistence vs. Derived Vector Storage
A foundational architectural invariant of HAMMER is the strict segregation between authoritative identity state and derived retrieval structures:
- **Authoritative System of Record:** A local SQLite database (`backend/academic_search.db`) manages relational tables for `source_records` (raw provider records), `publications` (canonical deduplicated entities), `duplicate_groups` (provenance groupings), `authors` (author master profiles), `author_candidates` (harvested profile pools), `author_resolutions` (persisted resolution decisions), and `author_resolution_evidence` (fine-grained feature signals). Transactional consistency and schema versioning are managed via SQLAlchemy ORM and Alembic migrations.
- **Derived Semantic Index:** ChromaDB (`backend/vector_index`) maintains a dense vector collection (`scholarly_records_v1`) containing 384-dimensional embeddings generated from publication titles and abstracts. ChromaDB is strictly downstream and rebuildable; it maintains zero authority over publication canonical IDs or author identities.

## 4. Technical Methodology

### 4.1 Publication Normalization and Deduplication (Engine V2)

#### 4.1.1 Legacy Deduplication Baseline (V1)
In the initial system prototype (V1), candidate publication records $r_1$ and $r_2$ were evaluated using a weighted linear combination of string and numerical similarities:
$$S_{V1}(r_1, r_2) = w_t \cdot \text{Sim}_{\text{title}}(r_1, r_2) + w_a \cdot \text{Sim}_{\text{author}}(r_1, r_2) + w_y \cdot \text{Sim}_{\text{year}}(r_1, r_2)$$
where $w_t = 0.60$, $w_a = 0.30$, and $w_y = 0.10$. A candidate pair was classified as a duplicate if $S_{V1}(r_1, r_2) \ge \theta_{\text{dedup}}$ (default $\theta_{\text{dedup}} = 0.85$). 

While effective for minor typographical variations, empirical failure analysis revealed that V1 suffered from severe false positive merges:
- **Edition Conflicts (FP1):** Revisions of foundational textbooks sharing identical authors and primary titles (e.g., Sutton and Barto's *Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction*, 1998 vs. 2018) scored $S_{V1} = 0.892$, exceeding the threshold and erroneously merging distinct editions into a single entity.
- **Semantically Distinct Keyword Overlap (FP2):** Independent publications sharing dense domain keywords (e.g., Deng and Yu's *Deep Learning: Methods and Applications* vs. Goodfellow et al.'s *Deep Learning*) generated false positive linkages.
- **Subtitle Truncations (FN1):** Preprint records lacking conference subtitles (e.g., Silver et al.'s *Mastering the Game of Go* vs. *Mastering the Game of Go with Deep Neural Networks and Tree Search*) scored $S_{V1} = 0.812$, failing the threshold and causing missed duplicate merges.

#### 4.1.2 Deduplication Engine V2 with Deterministic Safeguards
To resolve these failure modes, Deduplication Engine V2 wraps the baseline string metrics with five deterministic veto and qualification safeguards:

1. **Hard DOI Conflict Veto:** If both records possess valid, non-null DOIs and $\text{DOI}(r_1) \neq \text{DOI}(r_2)$, the records are classified as non-matches immediately, vetoing any merge regardless of title or author similarity:
   $$\text{Decision}(r_1, r_2) = \text{Non-Match} \quad \text{if } \text{DOI}(r_1) \neq \emptyset \land \text{DOI}(r_2) \neq \emptyset \land \text{DOI}(r_1) \neq \text{DOI}(r_2)$$
2. **Edition Conflict Detection:** Titles are scanned for edition markers (e.g., "1st edition", "2nd edition", "second edition"). If conflicting edition markers are detected, merging is strictly vetoed.
3. **Multi-Year Divergence Guard:** If the publication years differ by four or more years ($|\text{Year}(r_1) - \text{Year}(r_2)| \ge 4$) and no identical persistent identifier is present, merge is vetoed to prevent conflating longitudinal re-editions.
4. **Prefix-Aware Matching:** For works where one title represents a clean truncated prefix of the other followed by a colon or dash (e.g., main title vs. title + subtitle), the match score is computed over the shared prefix tokens, recovering true duplicates previously lost to subtitle truncation.
5. **Title Distinctness Floor:** A hard floor of $\text{Sim}_{\text{title}}(r_1, r_2) \ge 0.82$ is enforced; pairs falling below this floor are rejected unless qualifying under the prefix-matching rule.

### 4.2 Author Candidate Generation and Evidence-Guided Resolution

#### 4.2.1 Candidate Harvesting Pipeline
Author resolution initiates with candidate harvesting against the OpenAlex author graph. Queries undergo Unicode NFC normalization, case folding, and whitespace compaction. To address common transliteration and naming variants, the candidate harvester applies:
- **Compound Surname Expansion:** Detecting prefixes (e.g., "van", "de", "Le") to query both concatenated and space-separated variants (e.g., "Yann LeCun" vs. "Yann Le Cun").
- **Institutional Guard Filtering:** Stripping institutional suffixes from author input strings to prevent corrupted name matching.
- **Multi-Strategy Query Aggregation:** Combining exact display-name filtering with primary alias lookups, eliminating zero-candidate retrieval dropouts.

#### 4.2.2 Multi-Signal Deterministic Evidence Scoring
For each harvested candidate profile $c \in \mathcal{C}$, HAMMER extracts five fine-grained evidence signals comparing candidate metadata with query context $q$:
1. **Name Similarity ($S_{\text{name}} \in [0, 1]$):** Normalized Jaro-Winkler distance between query string and candidate display name / known aliases (weight $w_1 = 0.35$).
2. **Institutional Affiliation Compatibility ($S_{\text{inst}} \in [0, 1]$):** Jaccard token overlap between candidate historical affiliations and query institutional context (weight $w_2 = 0.25$).
3. **Publication Corpus Overlap ($S_{\text{pub}} \in [0, 1]$):** Overlap ratio between candidate indexed works and query publication context (weight $w_3 = 0.20$).
4. **Coauthor Network Overlap ($S_{\text{co}} \in [0, 1]$):** Shared coauthors between candidate graph and query context (weight $w_4 = 0.10$).
5. **Temporal Compatibility ($S_{\text{temp}} \in [0, 1]$):** Gaussian decay penalty over publication year divergence (weight $w_5 = 0.10$).

The aggregate candidate score is computed as:
$$S(c, q) = \sum_{i=1}^5 w_i \cdot S_i(c, q), \quad \sum_{i=1}^5 w_i = 1.0$$

#### 4.2.3 Non-Linear Decision Gates and Safety Abstention
Unlike probabilistic frameworks that assign candidate identities via uncalibrated argmax selection, HAMMER enforces non-linear decision gates to classify queries into three mutually exclusive operational states:

Let $c_{(1)}$ and $c_{(2)}$ represent the top-scoring and second-highest scoring candidates, with scores $S_1 = S(c_{(1)}, q)$ and $S_2 = S(c_{(2)}, q)$. A candidate identity is classified as **`auto_selected`** if and only if all four of the following conditions are simultaneously satisfied:
1. **Absolute Score Threshold:** $S_1 \ge \theta_{\text{score}}$ (where $\theta_{\text{score}} = 0.75$).
2. **Score Margin Gate:** $\Delta S = (S_1 - S_2) \ge \theta_{\text{margin}}$ (where $\theta_{\text{margin}} = 0.15$).
3. **Dominance Ratio Gate:** $R_{\text{dom}} = \frac{S_1}{\max(S_2, 0.001)} \ge \theta_{\text{dom}}$ (where $\theta_{\text{dom}} = 1.35$).
4. **Minimum Evidence Signals:** The candidate must exhibit non-zero evidence in at least $N_{\text{sig}} \ge 2$ independent signal categories.

If $S_1 \ge 0.45$ but the candidate fails the margin, dominance, or signal gates, the system abstains from automatic resolution and outputs **`manual_selection_required`**, presenting the ranked candidate pool and extracted evidence features to the user interface. If $S_1 < 0.45$ or the candidate pool is empty, the identity is designated as **`unresolved`**.

### 4.3 Dense Semantic Retrieval and Rebuildable Indexing
For contextual literature discovery, publication records in SQLite are mirrored into ChromaDB using the `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` bi-encoder model, which maps titles and abstracts into a 384-dimensional Euclidean space. Vector distance $d$ is converted to cosine similarity via:
$$\text{Sim}_{\text{dense}}(q, d) = 1.0 - \frac{d(q, d)}{2.0}$$
To prevent false positive retrievals on gibberish or out-of-domain queries, an empirical similarity floor $\tau = 0.35$ is enforced during top-$k$ approximate nearest neighbor search.

---

### 4.4 Synthesis and Zero-Trust Citation Containment
When user queries request an evidence synthesis summary, HAMMER optionally invokes Google Gemini 2.5 Flash under a zero-trust system prompt. The prompt provides at most eight retrieved publications formatted as strictly untrusted JSON context. The model is constrained via JSON schema to output:
1. `summary`: A concise synthesis paragraph.
2. `claims`: An array of individual factual assertions, each accompanied by an explicit list of cited `document_ids`.

Prior to presenting the synthesis to the user, a deterministic **Citation ID Containment Validator** inspects every claim. Let $\mathcal{D}_{\text{context}}$ denote the set of document identifiers supplied in the prompt context. For each claim $C$ with cited identifiers $\mathcal{D}_C$:
$$\text{Valid}(C) \iff \mathcal{D}_C \subseteq \mathcal{D}_{\text{context}} \land \mathcal{D}_C \neq \emptyset$$
Any claim citing an identifier outside $\mathcal{D}_{\text{context}}$ or fabricating a DOI is instantly excised. If parsing fails or all claims are discarded, the system reverts to a deterministic fallback summary compiled directly from SQLite metadata.

## 6. Discussion and Methodological Insights

### 6.1 The Value of Safety Abstention in Entity Resolution
A central finding of this research is that in academic entity resolution, **abstention is an intentional, high-value feature rather than a system defect**. Autonomous disambiguation frameworks that enforce hard clustering across all inputs inevitably corrupt author profiles when confronting homonyms with sparse coauthorship histories. By tuning decision gates to an uncompromising precision standard (yielding 13/13 verified automatic resolutions) and routing the remaining 52 ambiguous cases to an interactive review interface, HAMMER protects downstream bibliometric indices from irreversible corruption while dramatically reducing human review burden.

### 6.2 Structural Defense vs. Semantic Entailment
Our evaluation of the LLM explanation layer underscores an important methodological distinction between *syntactic citation containment* and *semantic entailment*. HAMMER's citation validator guarantees that an LLM cannot introduce external, fabricated document identifiers into an explanation. However, syntactic containment alone does not prove that the cited document semantically supports the specific assertion made in the generated text. Establishing genuine factual entailment requires blinded human multi-annotator review, which represents a critical priority for future work.

---

## 7. Threats to Validity and Limitations

1. **Benchmark Sample Size:** While Benchmark V3 (100 queries) and the Deduplication Benchmark (50 pairs) were curated to represent complex boundary conditions, they remain modest in scale compared to bulk digital library corpora. Expanding the test suite to thousands of pairs across diverse scientific domains is necessary to validate global generalization.
2. **External Knowledge Graph Dependency:** Candidate generation relies on the coverage and cleanliness of the OpenAlex graph. As demonstrated by the four non-matches in the All-Gold tier, split author profiles or delayed index synchronization in OpenAlex can limit candidate harvesting recall for rare or initial-only queries.
3. **Absence of Blinded Semantic Relevance Pools:** The semantic retrieval evaluation established effective gibberish rejection floors on 20 queries, but lacked large-scale, multi-annotator graded relevance pools (0, 1, 2) required to compute formal Information Retrieval metrics such as nDCG@k or Mean Average Precision (MAP).
4. **Scraper Brittleness:** Connectors targeting unstructured web sources (e.g., Google Scholar) remain highly vulnerable to anti-bot defenses, IP rate limits, and DOM schema mutations, reinforcing our architectural decision to disable scraping by default in favor of structured API protocols.

---

## 8. Conclusion and Future Work

This paper presented **HAMMER**, a hybrid, evidence-guided architecture for multi-source academic metadata aggregation, publication deduplication, and author entity resolution. By enforcing a strict architectural boundary between an authoritative relational database (SQLite) and rebuildable vector indices (ChromaDB), HAMMER ensures complete auditability and data integrity. On audited benchmarks, HAMMER achieved 100% precision and recall on decided publication deduplication pairs, 100% candidate retrieval recall on confirmed author identities, and 100% precision across automatic resolution decisions, while intercepting ungrounded generative citations.

Future work will focus on:
1. Scaling the deduplication and author benchmarks to multi-thousand pair corpora across biomedical (PubMed) and humanities domains.
2. Conducting multi-annotator double-blinded human evaluations to assess semantic entailment and claim faithfulness in LLM-generated explanations.
3. Incorporating graph neural network embeddings into the offline candidate harvesting pipeline to improve split-profile discovery for rare transliterated names.
