## Slide 3 literature table: item-level extraction

1. **Deep author name disambiguation using DBLP data.** The table attributes the work to Boukhers and Asundi and describes Char2Vec plus BERT, with coauthor/research-domain evidence. The model description is broadly supported by the paper. Its authors explicitly discuss handling new authors as a limitation; the slide’s closed-set note has support, but its scalability wording needs a specific source passage before use.
2. **PubMed Computed Authors in 2024.** The slide credits Tian et al. and mentions machine learning and ORCID. The publication is an open PubMed resource updated regularly and evaluates ORCID-linked records and GSD1900. Its biomedical domain is a scope limit; “limited explainability” is not established as a paper-stated limitation in the reviewed source.
3. **Name Disambiguation Scheme Based on Heterogeneous Academic Sites.** The slide attributes it to Li et al.; the actual authors are Choi et al. It integrates heterogeneous site metadata and selects among rule-based/learning schemes via a multi-classifier. The slide’s “no fusion strategy” claim is contradicted by the paper. The paper says the evaluation data are not publicly available because they were provided by a company.
4. **Author name disambiguation literature review with consolidated meta-analytic approach.** The slide credits Kumar et al.; the actual authors are Rodrigues, Mariano and Ralha. It is a review of 211 Web of Science/Scopus documents covering 2003–2022, not a software framework. The authors describe their review as exploratory rather than a systematic review with a pre-defined protocol.
5. **“Classical Rule-Based AND Methods.”** This is not a uniquely identifiable paper. “Ferreira et al.” and “Han et al.” without title, year or venue are insufficient to verify or cite. Do not manufacture a bibliography entry.
6. **AggAND.** The table credits Torvik and Smalheiser; they are not the authors of this 2021 paper. The paper is by Li Zhang, Yong Huang, Jinqing Yang and Wei Lu. It aggregates external scholarly databases to enrich PubMed author metadata and evaluates an author-disambiguation method.
7. **AffilGood.** The slide cites Duran-Silva et al. and describes institution name disambiguation. The paper provides datasets and tools for affiliation-span extraction, organization/location NER and ROR entity linking. “No department-level resolution” is a boundary of the paper’s organization-linking task, not evidence that the work failed its stated objective.
8. **From Raw Affiliations to Organization Identifiers.** The slide dates this as 2024; the proceedings paper is first online in 2025. It introduces AffRo and an expert-curated AffRoDB for strings with multiple organizations and extraneous text.
9. **Deduplicator.** The slide cites Forbes et al. (2024), title and general duplicate-record task correctly. It is a systematic-review workflow study, not an institutional dashboard study. That application distinction should not be presented as a scientific defect.
10. **Slide 11 reference 9: Reference Coverage Analysis of OpenAlex Compared to Web of Science and Scopus.** The preprint appeared in 2024; the version of record is Scientometrics 2025. It studies reference coverage and selected metadata, not author disambiguation.
11. **Slide 11 reference 10: Comparison of datasets citation coverage in Google Scholar, Web of Science, Scopus, Crossref, and DataCite.** The exact title says “datasets,” plural. It studies dataset-citation coverage for NASA EOSDIS, not generic bibliographic record aggregation.

## Cross-slide evidence conflicts to preserve

- Slide 10 presents three performance figures as HAMMER evaluation outputs; slides 14–15 say no sealed/labeled benchmark or controlled result existed. No date/version/scope note inside the deck resolves the conflict.
- “92% accuracy (Top-1 baseline)” is not the same metric as pairwise classification accuracy on GSD1900. The 2024 PubMed Computed Authors article reports 0.922 on a 400-pair test set and 0.910 on all 1,900 pairs; it reports no 88% abstention result in the cited table.
- The later Git V3 author artifact has a different query set and outcome design: 100 author-resolution queries, 56 gold-labeled identities, 44 controls, and outcomes auto-selected/manual/unresolved. These values must not be merged with the slide’s GSD1900 cards.
- The presentation’s earlier system architecture, technology stack, scope and conclusions are historical descriptions, not substitutes for the audited Git version.
- The deck’s row about classic methods is a citation gap, not an identifiable extra paper.
