## C. Working synthesis: evidence-led argument

Author name disambiguation is a mature entity-resolution problem with several operational forms: clustering existing publication mentions, assigning a new paper to an established profile, and deciding that the evidence is insufficient or a new identity is plausible. The review by Rodrigues et al. documents similarity/clustering and assignment/classification families; S2AND and WhoIsWho show that benchmark construction, dataset shift, scale and evaluation protocol are substantive research concerns. HAMMER should therefore avoid presenting author disambiguation itself, multi-source metadata, five hand-selected signals, or human review as novel.

Prior systems use overlapping identity evidence: name variants, coauthors, titles/topics, affiliations, publication context and external IDs. DBLP deep AND uses coauthor and title/source representations; AggAND supplements PubMed with external databases; Choi et al. adapt the chosen disambiguation scheme to the metadata available. AffilGood and AffRo further show that institutional parsing and organization linking are established, separable subtasks. The literature supports studying how these signals are retained and evaluated in HAMMER’s integrated workflow; it does not establish that its particular signal weights or decision thresholds are better.

The defensible research question is narrower: under a frozen candidate pool and independently sourced labels, how does HAMMER’s evidence-gated resolver trade automatic coverage against errors compared with a simple top-ranked choice, and what proportion of cases is routed to review or left unresolved? This connects to selective prediction’s risk–coverage framing and to existing human-in-the-loop author systems. It also matches HAMMER’s explicit state machine. The V3 artifact is a useful internal starting point, but its candidate-retrieval and resolver-decision records are not fully aligned. Re-scoring the committed Day 5 candidate-ID lists against the corrected V3 gold labels reproduces top-1 hits for 52/56 identities (50/50 Confirmed, 2/6 Probable; MRR 0.9286), with 56/56 non-empty candidate lists. Yet the V3 rows’ stored top_cand differs from the Day 5 rank-1 candidate for 30/56 gold queries, so the stored 13/52/35 resolver outcome distribution cannot be presented as a decision replay on that same production candidate snapshot. The gold set is curated, one of 13 auto-selected labels is only Probable, and candidate generation depends on OpenAlex. These boundaries must be shown rather than hidden.

Publication deduplication is related record linkage but is a different target: whether two records describe one work, not whether two author mentions describe one person. Fellegi–Sunter provides foundational linkage context, and Deduplicator shows a modern review-workflow comparator. HAMMER may include deduplication as a secondary systems question only after paired per-record V1/V2 predictions, labels and the committed edition detector are reconciled. A single aggregate score is not enough to support a comparative claim.

Cross-source metadata integration is not a field-wide absence. AggAND, OpenAlex, the source-coverage comparisons and PubMed Computed Authors provide counterexamples. A bounded systems contribution may examine source provenance and the consequences of source-specific completeness for a single reviewer-facing workflow, but no claim of first or unique integration is supported by the current audit.

## D. Provisional thesis boundaries

**Supportable framing:** HAMMER is an applied scholarly-metadata system that separates work-level matching from person-level resolution, retains source evidence, and uses conservative decision gates with review and unresolved outcomes. The paper can test the coverage/error trade-off of those gates on a bounded, explicitly described benchmark.

**Do not claim from the current evidence:** field-wide absence of multi-source integration; first author resolver with human review; open-world resolution novelty; guaranteed zero false merges; general accuracy from the October slide; semantic retrieval improving identity decisions; “explainability” proving a prediction true; broad multilingual, temporal, scalable, real-time or cross-domain performance.

## E. RQ/section alignment

- **Primary RQ2:** author candidate retrieval and selective identity decisions. Related work: AND methods and signals; benchmarks; open-set/reject-option/HITL. Methods must separate retrieval recall from resolver selective accuracy/coverage.
- **Conditional secondary RQ1:** work-level deduplication safeguards versus the legacy method. Related work: classical record linkage and publication deduplication. Include only after per-pair V1/V2 replay and bug correction.
- **Defer as research questions:** semantic retrieval ranking and LLM explanation quality. Existing feature/safety artifacts do not establish comparative ranking quality or semantic support.
- **Supporting architecture only:** provenance, OpenAlex source integration, affiliation resolution and optional search/explanation.

## F. Citation and research integrity notes

1. Cite the original GSD1900 paper and the specific table/denominator whenever discussing its scores.
2. Keep source-version years separate: online/preprint year and version-of-record year can differ.
3. Compare HAMMER only to studies with aligned task definition, label source, split, unit of analysis and metric.
4. Distinguish “reported by the paper,” “verified in Git,” “computed from frozen raw rows,” and “proposed.” A hash or internal audit report improves traceability but does not make a result peer-reviewed.
5. Reuse existing repository claim/reference registers; this note is additive and does not replace the historical Markdown draft or claim matrix.
