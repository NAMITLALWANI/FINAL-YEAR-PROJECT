# Slide 4 gap reassessment and citation-to-claim map

**Basis:** ten gap statements summarized from slide 4 of the October evaluation presentation; literature checked against original publisher/proceedings records; implementation assessed from committed Git HEAD d41378c and repository artifacts. “Partly implemented” describes code presence, not research validation.

## A. Reassessment of the ten presentation gaps

| # | Presentation gap | Literature reassessment | HAMMER at committed Git HEAD | Classification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fragmented metadata / lack of unified multi-source scholarly records | Broad absence is false. AggAND aggregates PubMed with external academic databases; OpenAlex is itself a multi-entity open scholarly graph; source-comparison studies document coverage differences. | Multiple connectors and normalization/provenance exist, but no controlled source-coverage comparison establishes better completeness or correctness. | **Not a field-wide gap; partially implemented system capability.** Possible bounded question: what provenance and source-specific coverage does the workflow preserve? |
| 2 | Closed-set author disambiguation cannot handle ambiguous or new authors | New-author assignment is an explicitly known limitation in prior supervised AND; WhoIsWho includes online assignment and task-specific evaluation; AMiner includes ongoing maintenance and human annotators. | Candidate generation and explicit auto/manual/unresolved states are present. This is not evidence of general open-set learning or reliable discovery of genuinely new identities. | **Established challenge; partially addressed operationally.** Evaluate negative/new-identity cases separately; do not claim novelty. |
| 3 | No temporal modeling | Temporal/career evidence is not absent from the literature. The slide’s field-wide claim is too broad. | Author scoring has a temporal-compatibility feature (weight 0.10 in the committed configuration), but not an evaluated longitudinal model of career change or temporal drift. | **Overstated gap; signal implemented, longitudinal behavior unvalidated.** |
| 4 | Existing systems fail when metadata are missing/sparse | Missing metadata is a known condition addressed in prior systems, including metadata-conditioned scheme selection in Choi et al. | The scorer re-normalizes available signal weights. No stratified missingness or ablation evaluation establishes robustness. | **Not novel as a gap; mechanism present, empirical robustness unverified.** |
| 5 | Domain bias in existing approaches | Benchmark bias and poor cross-dataset transfer are documented in S2AND; cross-domain evaluation is a real validity concern. | HAMMER’s main author source and curated examples are not a cross-domain benchmark; no stratified discipline result is established. | **Valid evaluation concern; unaddressed empirically.** A defensible gap is an evaluation need in this project, not a universal lack in the field. |
| 6 | Scalability and class imbalance | Large-scale approaches already exist: AMiner reports large deployment; WhoIsWho has over one million papers. Prior approaches also explicitly address class imbalance/small author clusters. | Repository has API services and batch artifacts, but no scale, throughput, class-imbalance or load study sufficient for a research claim. | **Field-wide absence is false; HAMMER performance question untested.** |
| 7 | Opaque decisions / lack of explainability | Explainability and review interfaces are established design choices; AMiner includes human annotation. Evidence display alone is not novel or proof of correctness. | Score contributions, reasons and evidence persistence are implemented. No blinded user study shows that reviewers understand or use the evidence correctly. | **Partly implemented; benefit not measured.** Call it evidence traceability/reviewer support unless a user study is conducted. |
| 8 | Duplicate publications create bad analytics | Publication linkage and duplicate-record detection are established; Fellegi–Sunter is foundational and Deduplicator studies a modern review workflow. | V1/V2 logic and a 50-pair aggregate exist. The report gives V2 TP=33, FP=0, TN=15, FN=0 over 48 scored pairs plus two boundary/uncertain pairs, but does not preserve a comparable per-pair V1/V2 prediction table. Committed engine also contains an edition-regex defect flagged in the Stage 0 audit. | **Established problem; conditional secondary RQ.** Correct the detector and replay both methods on identical frozen pairs before reporting a comparative result. |
| 9 | Affiliations are hard to normalize to department level | AffilGood and AffRo already address organization extraction/linking and complex affiliation strings. Organization ID linking is not equivalent to department hierarchy resolution. | HAMMER has token-based institution compatibility/normalization, but no demonstrated ROR link or department-level resolver. | **Established subtask, but novelty claim false; partially implemented.** Treat affiliation as one identity signal and state the department-level boundary. |
| 10 | Real-time updates are missing | Online/incremental assignment and update workflows exist in prior systems, including WhoIsWho’s daily arXiv assignment and PubMed Computed Authors’ updates. | Async APIs, caches, circuit breakers and incremental index upserts exist. There is no freshness SLA, long-running drift test or comparative end-to-end latency result. | **Field-wide absence false; engineering features present, operational performance unvalidated.** |

## B. Citation-to-claim map

| Proposed paper claim | Primary support | What the source supports | Boundary / phrasing control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scholarly AND is a mature, difficult entity-resolution task with varied methods and datasets. | [Rodrigues et al. 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00799-024-00398-1); [S2AND](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07534); [WhoIsWho](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11848) | Taxonomy, benchmark variability, scale and dataset-shift concerns. | Do not say “no prior solution” or cite one benchmark as universal ground truth. |
| Author evidence can combine names, affiliations, coauthors, publication/topic context and external identifiers. | [Boukhers & Asundi](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00799-023-00361-6); [AggAND](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8363810/); [PubMed Computed Authors](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11588201/) | Specific prior systems use these signals and data sources. | These papers do not validate HAMMER’s feature weights or establish that this signal set is novel. |
| Multi-source author disambiguation has prior art. | [AggAND](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8363810/); [Choi et al.](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/14/1/192); [OpenAlex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01833) | External metadata enrichment, heterogeneous-site metadata and an open scholarly graph. | Do not claim HAMMER is the first unified multi-source platform. |
| Data source completeness varies by source and task. | [Culbert et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16359); [Gerasimov et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11192-024-05073-5) | Comparative reference/metadata coverage in explicitly sampled corpora. | The studies do not establish global source rankings or author-identity accuracy. |
| New-author assignment, ambiguity and human escalation are relevant operating conditions. | [Boukhers & Asundi](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00799-023-00361-6); [AMiner HITL](https://www.kdd.org/kdd2018/accepted-papers/view/name-disambiguation-in-aminer-clustering-maintenance-and-human-in-the-loop); [WhoIsWho](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11848) | Prior new-author limitations, human annotation and online assignment. | HAMMER’s review/unresolved states are design behavior; measure their accuracy and workload separately. |
| Selective decisions should be reported as error/risk at coverage. | [SelectiveNet](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/geifman19a.html) | A general selective-prediction/reject-option framework and risk–coverage evaluation. | This is general ML theory, not an AND result; do not transfer performance claims. |
| Institution linking is a distinct, established task. | [AffilGood](https://aclanthology.org/2024.sdp-1.13/); [AffRo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07577) | Affiliation-span, entity and organization-ID subtasks. | Organization ID resolution is not department hierarchy resolution or person identity. |
| Publication deduplication differs from author resolution. | [Fellegi–Sunter](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1969.10501049); [Deduplicator](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13643-024-02619-9) | Record linkage theory and work-level duplicate-record workflow evaluation. | Do not treat a publication match as proof that two author mentions are the same person. |
| GSD1900 is an external pairwise benchmark, not a HAMMER result. | [Vishnyakova et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7647200/); [Tian et al. 2024 results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11588201/) | Original 1,900-pair MEDLINE gold standard; later paper’s reported pairwise results. | Cite exact subset and denominator. Do not label 92.2% pairwise accuracy as HAMMER top-1 accuracy. |
| HAMMER is novel, first, uniquely safe, or improves quality over prior work. | No external source can substantiate a novelty claim by itself. | A novelty claim requires a scoped prior-art search plus a supported contribution statement. | **Do not claim** until scope and comparative method are approved and evidence is produced. |
